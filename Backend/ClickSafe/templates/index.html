Understanding URLs: Legitimate vs Phishing

A simple guide to identifying safe and malicious web addresses

What is a URL?

A URL (Uniform Resource Locator) is the web address you type or click to visit a website. Every URL has distinct parts — understanding them helps you quickly spot whether a link is safe or dangerous.

https://www.example.com/account?id=123

Legitimate URLs

Legitimate URLs come from trusted, recognizable sources. They use correct spelling, HTTPS, and a clean domain that matches the real organization — nothing extra, nothing suspicious.

https://www.google.com/search ✓ Known brand, HTTPS, simple domain
https://paypal.com/signin ✓ Exact brand domain, no extra words
https://amazon.com/orders ✓ Official domain, no suspicious subdomains
https://mail.google.com/inbox ✓ Official subdomain of google.com
Phishing URLs

Phishing URLs are crafted to look like real sites but trick you into giving up passwords or installing malware. They often misspell domains or hide the real brand name inside a fake one.

http://google.com.login-verify.net/ ✗ Real domain is login-verify.net, not google.com
https://paypa1.com/secure-login ✗ "l" replaced with "1" — typosquatting
http://amaz0n-account-update.com ✗ HTTP only, fake domain with hyphens & zero
https://secure-gmail-login.xyz/auth ✗ Gmail never uses .xyz or third-party domains

## How to Spot a Phishing URL
*
Check for HTTPS — but note that HTTPS alone doesn't guarantee safety; it just encrypts the connection.
*
The real domain is the part just before the first single slash. In evil.com/paypal, the domain is evil.com, not PayPal.
*
Be suspicious of urgency language in links — "verify now", "account suspended", "click immediately".
*
Don't click links in emails. Type the official URL directly in your browser or use a saved bookmark.
*
Hover over any link before clicking — your browser shows the real destination in the status bar at the bottom.
*
Watch for numbers replacing letters: paypa1.com, g00gle.com, amaz0n.com.
